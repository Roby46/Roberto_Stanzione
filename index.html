About

I received my Ph.D. at the University of Salerno, where my research project focused on data profiling and the definition of metadata-driven approaches for data management tasks. My work focused on a specific type of multi-attribute constraint, i.e., Relaxed Functional Dependencies (RFDs). My research examined the use of RFDs for data imputation, query relaxation, and concept drift detection.

I am currently a postdoc researcher at École Normale Supérieure, where my research focuses on time series analysis and the application of foundation models and large language models (LLMs) to temporal data. The latter includes the study of time series representations for effectively providing temporal information to foundation models.

I am also interested in time series anomaly detection, segmentation, and motif discovery, with a particular focus on understanding the capabilities and limitations of traditional methods, foundation models, and human experts across these tasks. Furthermore, my research explores the use of LLMs to support the interpretation of anomalies in time series. I also continue to investigate data quality in time series and its implications for downstream analysis.
